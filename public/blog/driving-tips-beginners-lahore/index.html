Learning to drive can feel overwhelming at first, but with the right tips and regular practice, anyone can become a confident driver. These are the tips Madam Razia shares with every new student at Razia Driving Center.

1. Master the Clutch First

Clutch control is the foundation of manual driving. Everything else — moving off, gear changes, hill starts, parking — depends on it. Spend your first few lessons really understanding the biting point. Once that becomes muscle memory, everything else gets easier.

Tip: Practice finding the biting point on flat ground with the handbrake on. Slowly lift the clutch until the car dips slightly — that is your biting point. Do this 20 times until you can find it without thinking.

2. Look Ahead, Not at the Hood

Beginners often stare at the hood or the road right in front of the car. Instead, look 10-15 seconds ahead. Your hands will naturally follow your eyes, and your steering will be much smoother.

3. Use Your Mirrors Constantly

Check your mirrors before every turn, lane change, and stop. Make it a habit from day one. Examiners and experienced drivers both watch for this. A quick glance takes less than a second and keeps you aware of everything around you.

4. Keep a Safe Following Distance

In Lahore traffic, the car ahead can stop suddenly. Keep at least a 2-second gap in normal traffic and more in heavy rain or rush hour. If someone cuts in front of you, just ease off and restore your gap.

5. Practice Parking Separately

Parking is a skill on its own. Practice parallel parking and bay parking until you can do them without stress. Use cones or bottles as markers if you are practicing in an empty lot.

Tip: For parallel parking, stop next to the car in front of the space. Reverse while turning the wheel toward the curb. When your car is at 45 degrees, straighten out and reverse into the space.

6. Don't Panic in Traffic

Lahore traffic can look chaotic, but most drivers follow predictable patterns. Stay calm, keep your speed low, and focus on your own driving. If you feel overwhelmed, pull over safely and take a breath.

7. Learn to Read the Road

Watch for:

  • Pedestrians stepping onto the road
  • Motorcycles weaving between cars
  • Parked cars that might pull out
  • Speed bumps and potholes
  • Traffic signals changing to yellow

8. Practice Hill Starts

Hill starts are one of the hardest skills for beginners. Use the handbrake method: hold the handbrake, find the biting point, then release the handbrake as you gently accelerate. Practice on a gentle slope first.

9. Drive at the Right Speed

Don't drive too slow — it can be just as dangerous as speeding. Match the traffic flow and stay within the speed limit. In residential areas, keep it under 30 km/h. On main roads, follow the posted limits.

10. Be Patient with Yourself

Everyone stalls, everyone makes mistakes. The difference between a good driver and a bad one is not talent — it is practice. Give yourself time, stay consistent with lessons, and you will get there.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get better at clutch control?

Practice finding the biting point on flat ground first, then on slight slopes. With daily practice, it becomes muscle memory within a week.

How do I park correctly as a beginner?

For parallel parking, align your car next to the vehicle in front of the space, reverse at a 45-degree angle, then straighten out. Practice with cones or markers first.

How do I stay calm in Lahore traffic?

Keep a safe distance from the car ahead, avoid rushing, and remember that the instructor has dual controls. Focus on your own driving and ignore aggressive drivers.

What is the most important skill for a beginner?

Clutch control is the most important skill. Once you can control the clutch smoothly, everything else becomes much easier.

How many lessons before driving alone?

Most beginners need 7 to 15 days of one-to-one lessons before they are confident enough to drive alone.
